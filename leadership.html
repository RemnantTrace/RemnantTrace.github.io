RemnantTrace Forensics was founded by Meg Sissel and Lauren Yost to conduct independent research into digital evidence, forensic methods, emerging technologies, and interdisciplinary scientific research.

01 / FOUNDER

Meg Sissel

FOUNDER & CEO

Meg Sissel is the Founder and CEO of RemnantTrace Forensics, an independent research laboratory focused on digital evidence, digital forensics, forensic methodology, and emerging technologies.

Her professional background spans software engineering, bioinformatics, security operations, technical training, incident-response workflow development, litigation support, and digital forensics. This multidisciplinary background informs RemnantTrace's approach to forensic research: examining not only what evidence exists, but how it is created, stored, modified, synchronized, recovered, and ultimately interpreted.

02 / BACKGROUND

Megan's technical and investigative background spans software engineering, bioinformatics, security operations, technical systems, incident response, and digital forensics. Her academic training in Software Engineering and Bioinformatics established a strong foundation in programming, computational systems, databases, and data analysis, which she has carried throughout her professional work in security and technology.

Her experience includes security operations, technical training, operational systems, incident-response workflows, and database development for live case-management environments. From 2023 to 2024, she served as a Technical Trainer supporting Volvo Trucks North America, Mack Trucks, Penta, and Prevost, where her work extended beyond technical instruction into workflow development and incident-response operations. She developed databases used to manage case workflows within live call-center environments and worked alongside Security Operations Center personnel and engineering teams. In 2025, she continued this work with Reliance Forensics, providing digital forensics and litigation support involving the examination, preservation, and analysis of digital evidence across criminal and civil matters.

Megan currently works as a Forensics Analyst at CyberNarus in Minnesota, continuing her professional work in digital forensics and digital evidence analysis. In 2026, she founded RemnantTrace Forensics as an independent research platform focused on digital evidence, forensic methodology, and emerging technologies. RemnantTrace provides a dedicated platform for conducting, documenting, and publishing independent research built around controlled experimentation, repeatable methods, and a deeper understanding of the technical conditions behind digital artifacts.

COFOUNDER & RESEARCH LEAD

Lauren Yost is a Co-Founder and Research Lead at RemnantTrace Forensics, where she leads scientific research development and contributes to the laboratory's interdisciplinary research initiatives involving emerging technologies, experimental methodology, and complex systems.

Lauren's academic background spans Biology and Anthropology, with additional graduate-level research at the University of Miami. Her scientific interests include primate epigenetics and the study of biological systems through experimental research and interdisciplinary scientific methods.

At RemnantTrace, Lauren focuses on research development, experimental design, scientific literature review, methodology, and reproducibility. She contributes to the development of research questions, experimental frameworks, documentation standards, and structured research outputs.

Her role brings a scientific research discipline to RemnantTrace that complements its work in digital forensics and technology. Lauren's expertise in biological and anthropological research strengthens the laboratory's ability to approach emerging questions through rigorous experimentation, interdisciplinary analysis, and evidence-based research.

04 / THE MISSION

Independent research into the traces technology leaves behind.

RemnantTrace exists to investigate questions that sit at the intersection of technology and forensic science.

The laboratory's work is centered on experimentation, reproducibility, documentation, and transparency. Research examines digital systems under defined conditions to better understand the evidence they produce and the limitations associated with interpreting that evidence.
